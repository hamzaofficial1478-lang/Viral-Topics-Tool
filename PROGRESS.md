- **OUTPUT QUALITY: pixelated clips traced to a 1080p DOWNLOAD CAP, not the encoder** · unverified (652 tests pass; measured on synthetic renders in-sandbox, not yet on the operator's footage): operator: *"the output quality is very low, the videos are pixelated... the quality gets down when I upload videos on Facebook."* Three independent causes, and the biggest one was upstream of the encoder entirely.
  1. **THE ROOT CAUSE — the source was never big enough.** `ingest.format` was hard-capped at `height<=1080`. Cropping a 1920x1080 download to 9:16 keeps the full height and cuts the width to `1080 x 0.5625` = **608 px**, which then has to be enlarged **1.78x** to reach 1080x1920. *No encoder setting recovers detail that was never downloaded.* New `resolution.source_height_needed()` states the rule — for a 16:9 source the needed source height simply equals the **output** height, so a 1080x1920 vertical clip needs a **1920px-tall (4K) source** — and `ingest.source_ceiling()` now sizes the download from what the chosen export actually needs (a 720p export pulls 1440p, not 4K), bounded by new `ingest.max_height` (default 2160) so an 8K source can't arrive by accident. Measured: same scene, same encoder, **3.70x** the acutance once the 4K source is available.
  2. **The deliverable was encoded at speed settings.** CRF 20 / preset **veryfast** — a compromise applied to the one artefact that Facebook, Instagram and YouTube then **re-encode again**, making your file the *input* to their encoder, where shipped artefacts get compounded.
